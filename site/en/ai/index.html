The Core Challenge: Decision-Making Under Geological Uncertainty

As exploration moves deeper, drilling costs rise, and so do the consequences of choosing the wrong targets. At the same time, the information available in early stages is rarely sufficient to determine the structure and extent of mineralization with certainty.

Under these conditions, the value of an exploration program depends on its ability to manage uncertainty, revise decisions as new evidence emerges and allocate capital effectively.

Three challenges stand out:

01

Uncertainty in Subsurface Understanding

Limited data can support several plausible interpretations of a deposit’s structure, geometry and properties.

02

Cost and Risk of Drilling

Drillholes must be selected so that the information they yield improves subsequent project decisions.

03

The Need for Continuous Program Review

Geological models and drilling priorities need to be updated as actual results arrive at each stage.

Proposed Framework: Probabilistic Modeling and Adaptive Decision-Making

The scientific basis of the program is to formulate mineral exploration as a sequential decision problem under limited observability of the subsurface.

Within this framework, probabilistic modeling, Bayesian inference and a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) will be used to evaluate drilling options and their possible outcomes.

The method has four main components:

  1. 1

    Simultaneous Modeling of Geological Hypotheses

    Rather than relying on a single deterministic interpretation, the model considers a set of plausible hypotheses about the structure and extent of mineralization.

    Probability distributions of subsurface properties will be defined for each hypothesis.

  2. 2

    Probabilistic Model Updating

    As new results arrive, the consistency of the observations with each hypothesis is assessed, and the hypotheses’ probabilistic weights are revised using Bayesian methods.

  3. 3

    Hypothesis Adequacy Assessment

    Beyond comparing the existing hypotheses, the model is intended to assess how well they explain the data.

    Using a reference model with no defined spatial structure, this process will help identify where the initial hypotheses need to be revisited.

  4. 4

    Drilling Planning Based on the Value of Information

    Drilling options are assessed according to cost, possible outcomes and the effect of the resulting information on future decisions.

    The primary criterion is maximizing the expected value of exploration decisions, not simply selecting the drillhole with the highest predicted grade.

03

Proposed Adaptive Drilling Planning Process

In the proposed architecture, the exploration program is a staged process in which the results of each drillhole inform the evaluation and selection of the next actions.

The process has five main stages:

Conceptual view of a probabilistic subsurface structure
01

Integration of Exploration Data

Geological, geophysical, geochemical and drilling data are prepared for analysis, taking into account their provenance, quality and level of uncertainty.

02

Probabilistic Subsurface Models

Alternative geological scenarios and probabilistic estimates of grade, thickness and extent of mineralization are defined.

03

Evaluation of Drilling Options

Drillhole locations and sequencing are assessed against the expected value of information, operating costs and the effect on future decisions.

04

Observation and Updating

New drilling results are compared with predictions, and estimates and hypothesis weights are revised.

05

Deciding the Next Stage

Continuing exploration, modifying the program, advancing to the next development stage or stopping the project is evaluated against the evidence and against technical and economic criteria.

After stage 4, results feed back into modeling (stage 2) and drilling planning (stage 3).
04

Expected Value for Mining Projects

The framework is designed to improve the quality of exploration decisions and the efficiency of capital deployment.

The program’s economic performance will be assessed in four areas:

Optimizing Exploration Capital

Assessing the potential to reduce low-yield work and to direct resources toward options with higher information value.

Improved Understanding of Mineral Resources

Greater capacity to evaluate geological scenarios and to present estimates together with their uncertainty ranges.

Decision Risk Management

Assessing the possible consequences of continuing drilling, modifying the program or stopping work at different project stages.

Support for Investment Evaluation

Providing analytical information for assessing mineral opportunities and comparing technical and economic alternatives.

Scientific Foundations & Technology Development

Our approach brings together probabilistic geological modeling, sequential decision-making and economic assessment within an integrated scientific framework for mineral exploration.

The conceptual architecture has been developed, and preliminary computational studies have examined its underlying decision-making logic. Full implementation and validation using real exploration data remain the next stages of development.
